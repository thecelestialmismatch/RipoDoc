**Decision supported:** Whether and how to pursue a controlled US/Australia MSP design-partner launch before broad product expansion.

## Market boundary and exclusions

> **Market definition:** RipoDoc addresses an MSP report-delivery moment in which a client challenges or needs to inspect a material report claim, and the MSP needs a reviewed, reader-bounded, auditable answer without disclosing a broad raw export or redirecting the recipient into a generic portal.

The research excludes PSA, RMM, ticketing, generic BI, generic QBR authoring, generic client-portal, data-room, procurement, or compliance-certification markets except where they serve as substitutes for the defined moment.

## Commercial hypotheses to test

1. MSPs experience a recurring, high-friction report-trust moment that cannot be handled adequately by an existing portal, report export, or manual email.
2. The decision-maker will pay for the bounded proof-delivery layer rather than considering it a feature of current MSP reporting software.
3. The initial ideal customer has enough report volume, account-management maturity, and client scrutiny to feel this pain, but not such an entrenched workflow that switching is impractical.
4. A short evidence-delivery teardown converts more effectively than a generic product demonstration.
5. Five paid, repeated-use design partners can establish the required product, pricing, and integration priorities more reliably than a broad self-serve launch.

## Research and implementation decision gates

| Gate | Evidence required | Decision if met | Decision if not met |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem frequency | At least 15 founder interviews; a documented recurring client-report challenge in at least 8 conversations | Continue discovery and paid-pilot offer | Reframe the job-to-be-done before expanding features. |
| Existing-workaround failure | At least 5 prospects describe a specific loss of control, exposure, delay, or rework with their current answer path | Prioritise delivery receipt and policy controls | Do not build more workflow before a distinct pain is demonstrated. |
| Paid conversion | At least 3 of 5 qualified design partners accept a paid pilot with a defined use case | Invest in production activation and product roadmap | Revisit price, ICP, or willingness-to-pay. |
| Repeat-use proof | At least 3 paid pilots use RipoDoc in a second reporting cycle | Build narrow source connectors and state-change automation | Do not broaden the product on single-use interest. |
| Source-system repetition | At least 3 paid pilots name the same authoritative source/system and same decision owner | Build that connector behind review controls | Maintain upload/manifest workflow; no speculative integrations. |
| Security/operations readiness | Real production preflight returns `ready`, controlled end-to-end tests pass, and backup/restore is proven | Accept controlled production customer data | Keep public product fail-closed. |

## Evidence standards

- Primary product and pricing pages, official vendor documentation, regulators, public statistics, and direct customer interviews outrank market commentary.
- Search snippets are discovery only; every decision-relevant factual claim must be verified against its source.
- Revenue, market-size, CAC, conversion, and sales-cycle figures are treated as **planning scenarios**, not RipoDoc facts, unless the owner provides source records.
- Competitors are assessed by documented scope and buyer workflow. A feature listed on a page does not prove customer adoption, satisfaction, or revenue.
- The final strategy will label facts, estimates, hypotheses, owner decisions, and unproven claims separately.
