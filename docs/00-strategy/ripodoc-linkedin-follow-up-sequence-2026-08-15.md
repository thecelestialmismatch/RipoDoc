# RipoDoc LinkedIn Follow-Up Sequence for MSP Founders

**Prepared:** 2026-08-15
**Audience:** RipoDoc founder and early MSP sales team
**Pair with:** `ripodoc-msp-founder-email-outreach-playbook-2026-08-15.md`
**Objective:** Continue a relevant founder-to-founder conversation after email outreach without creating repetitive, automated, or pressure-based contact.

> **Operating rule:** Use LinkedIn for a thoughtful continuation, not a second automated blast. Send only when the person and company are a plausible fit, the personal detail is public and relevant, and the message can stand on its own if they never saw the email.

## 1. Positioning to keep stable

Use the same statement in every channel:

> **RipoDoc is controlled evidence delivery for MSP client reports.** It is for the moment a client asks where a selected report claim came from. It is not a PSA, RMM, BI tool, general client portal, compliance certification, arbitrary PDF extractor, or autonomous AI publishing system.

Do not write “we guarantee retention,” “we make QBRs compliant,” “we replace your reporting stack,” “AI proves your reports,” or any other claim the product cannot substantiate. The offer is a short **report teardown**: one claim, one source, one reader, one review boundary, and one next decision.

## 2. Before the first message

Use only 60–90 seconds of factual preparation. Read a public company page, a founder post, or a service page. Record one observation that is public, accurate, and connected to the reporting conversation.

| Personalization field | Acceptable example | Do not use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{{first_name}}` | Jordan | Nicknames not used publicly. |
| `{{msp_name}}` | Northpoint IT | A guessed company name. |
| `{{public_observation}}` | “You describe quarterly technology roadmaps for clients on your services page.” | Private information, scraped personal data, or false praise. |
| `{{reporting_context}}` | “That often creates the question of how a client can inspect a metric without receiving a broad export.” | “You probably struggle with reporting.” |
| `{{email_reference}}` | “I sent a short note on the same question last week.” | “Did you see my email?” |

Stop before messaging if no public, relevant observation exists. A generic note is worse than no note.

## 3. Message sequence

### Touch 1 — Connection request

**Timing:** Same day as the first email or the next business day.
**Goal:** Establish relevance without pitching a meeting.

```text
Hi {{first_name}} — I noticed {{public_observation}}.

I’m building RipoDoc around a narrow MSP report question: when a client asks where one reported metric came from, how do you answer without forwarding a broad export?

No pitch in the connection note. I’d value connecting with people who already run these conversations.
```

**Alternative for a highly relevant founder post:**

```text
Hi {{first_name}} — your point about {{specific_public_topic}} stood out.

I’m researching the point where a client-facing report needs a defensible answer behind one claim, not another dashboard. Would be glad to connect.
```

Do not include a link in the connection request.

### Touch 2 — First post-acceptance message

**Timing:** One to two business days after acceptance.
**Goal:** Ask a single diagnostic question and offer the public sample as an optional inspection point.

```text
Thanks for connecting, {{first_name}}.

A narrow question I’m testing with MSP founders: when a client asks about a number in a QBR or service report, does the answer usually become a spreadsheet, screenshot, wider portal access, or a manual data chase?

RipoDoc is designed for that moment only: a selected claim with reviewed, bounded support for the intended reader. The public sample uses fixture data, so there is nothing to upload: https://www.ripodoc.com/#sample-report

If that question is not relevant to {{msp_name}}, no need to reply.
```

### Touch 3 — Value-led follow-up

**Timing:** Five to seven business days after Touch 2, only if there is no reply.
**Goal:** Deliver a useful self-assessment rather than restate the product.

```text
One practical check I’ve been using for this report moment:

1. What exact claim could a client challenge?
2. Which reviewed source supports it?
3. What is the least data the reader needs?
4. Who approves that boundary?
5. What access condition should apply?

If any answer is unclear, the current report may be carrying more trust than its proof path. I wrote the MSP version of the guide here: {{starter_kit_url}}.

No form and no customer data needed.
```

Until the starter-kit page is live, omit the final link and offer to send the five questions in a reply. Do not link to an unavailable diagnostic form as if it captures a request.

### Touch 4 — Report teardown invitation

**Timing:** Seven to ten business days after Touch 3, only for a strong-fit prospect.
**Goal:** Convert a specific problem into a bounded conversation.

```text
{{first_name}}, I’ll make this concrete rather than keep sending notes.

If {{msp_name}} has one recurring report claim that gets challenged, I can do a 15-minute teardown: claim, source, reader, review boundary, and current workaround. We use public, approved, or redacted examples only — never credentials, client exports, PHI/ePHI, or sensitive production data.

The useful outcome can be “this is not a RipoDoc problem.” Would a short teardown be worthwhile?
```

### Touch 5 — Permissioned close-the-loop

**Timing:** Ten to fourteen business days after Touch 4, only if there is no reply.
**Goal:** End respectfully and preserve a future relationship.

```text
I’ll close the loop after this, {{first_name}}.

RipoDoc is deliberately narrow: controlled evidence delivery behind a selected MSP client-report claim. If a report question ever becomes a broad export or a slow data chase, the five-question teardown is available.

If it is not relevant, no action needed. I won’t keep following up here.
```

## 4. Reply handling

| Reply type | Recommended response | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “We use {{portal/BI/reporting tool}}.” | “That may already solve the broader reporting job. I’m not asking you to replace it. Has a client ever needed the basis for one published claim, and did the existing tool provide an appropriately scoped answer?” | Continue only if they identify a separate proof-boundary problem. |
| “Send more information.” | “The clearest material is the fixture-data claim sample and the five-question starter kit. The key question is whether a selected claim needs reviewed, reader-appropriate support—not whether another dashboard is useful.” | Send only the relevant resource; ask one question. |
| “We do not have this problem.” | “That is useful to know. It suggests your report process or client expectations already provide a sufficient answer. I will not keep pushing it.” | Stop sequence; record disqualifier. |
| “We are interested.” | “Great. Before a call, can you name the report type, one claim that has been challenged, the current source, and who approves client-facing detail? Please keep client names and data out of the reply.” | Schedule a report teardown using redacted/approved context only. |
| “Is this compliant / HIPAA-ready?” | “RipoDoc does not certify compliance or replace your responsibilities. Any regulated use requires a separate scope, deployment, contractual, security, and data-handling review before production data is involved.” | Do not promise; follow the documented healthcare/regulated boundary. |
| “Can AI build this?” | “RipoDoc does not treat unreviewed AI output as authority. The first workflow is selected claim → reviewed source → bounded evidence → human approval. AI assistance is not a substitute for that boundary.” | Re-center on current product scope. |

## 5. Cadence and channel rules

| Rule | Why it protects the campaign |
| --- | --- |
| Send no more than one LinkedIn follow-up per seven days after acceptance. | Avoids repetitive pressure and leaves room for the person to respond. |
| Stop after Touch 5 or any explicit disinterest. | Protects reputation and keeps the outreach permissioned. |
| Do not use connection-automation tools, mass scraping, copied endorsements, or fake mutual-context statements. | The campaign should create genuine research conversations, not synthetic activity. |
| Do not ask for client names, customer reports, credentials, PHI/ePHI, or operational exports in LinkedIn messages. | Preserves the public-data and regulated-data boundary. |
| Record the actual objection and current workaround after every meaningful reply. | The learning is more valuable than a vanity response rate. |
| Use one channel as the active follow-up channel at a time. | If the prospect replies by email, stop the LinkedIn sequence and continue the conversation there. |

## 6. Founder workflow and scorecard

Run outreach in small, manually reviewed cohorts of 10–15 high-fit founders. The goal is not a high connection count. It is to identify report owners with an actual evidence-boundary problem.

| Field to record | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `Fit: yes / no / unknown` | Whether recurring reports and a report owner exist. |
| `Challenged claim: yes / no` | Whether the prospect can name a real example. |
| `Current workaround` | Spreadsheet, screenshot, portal access, manual data chase, or already solved. |
| `Source readiness` | Reviewed structured source, unclear source, or arbitrary PDF only. |
| `Owner` | Person who can approve client-facing evidence. |
| `Next decision` | Teardown, no fit, later, or follow-up by another channel. |
| `Explicit opt-out / disinterest` | Stop immediately. |

The sequence is working when conversations reveal specific report moments and disqualify poor fits quickly. It is not working merely because people accept connection requests or click a sample link.
