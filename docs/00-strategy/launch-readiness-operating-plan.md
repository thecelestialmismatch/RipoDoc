# RipoDoc Launch-Readiness Operating Plan

**Prepared:** 2026-08-15  
**Status:** Operating plan. Commercial terms and final legal documents remain subject to accountable business-owner and counsel approval.

> **Legal note:** I’m an AI, not a lawyer — this is a working launch analysis, not formal legal advice. A qualified attorney must approve the final seller identity, target jurisdictions, commercial terms, privacy/cookie notice, refund policy, DPA, SLA, and healthcare posture before reliance or broad paid hosted launch.

## 1. The market thesis to test

RipoDoc should not compete as “another reporting platform.” MSP QBR guidance consistently values concise business context, strategic conversation, transparent evidence, and follow-up action—not report volume.[1] The commercial thesis is therefore deliberately narrow:

> **RipoDoc is the controlled evidence-delivery layer for the report an MSP already sends. A selected approved claim can lead the intended reader to bounded supporting evidence without turning the QBR into a raw-data dump.**

This is a testable product thesis. It does not claim that reports improve retention, reduce preparation time, provide compliance, or replace a PSA/RMM/BI/client portal until RipoDoc has permissioned customer evidence.

## 2. Positioning and message hierarchy

| Audience | Their job | Message that can be tested | Evidence to show in the demo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MSP owner | Retain clients and protect margin | “Make the value in your existing report inspectable when a client challenges a claim.” | Open a sample report; select a claim; reveal only its support. |
| vCIO / account manager | Lead a strategic conversation | “Keep the meeting focused. Answer ‘show me the data’ without emailing a broad export.” | Claim-to-evidence view, explanatory context, and action. |
| service-delivery lead | Produce a repeatable review | “Review the source, approve the claim, and publish a controlled proof path.” | Review boundary, sample source/provenance, and publication record. |
| security-minded buyer | Limit unnecessary sharing | “Deliver the evidence relevant to one claim rather than an unrestricted spreadsheet.” | Recipient-aware access, expiry/revocation concept, and audit-aware delivery record. |

### Explicit boundaries

RipoDoc must continue to say what it does **not** do: it is not an arbitrary PDF extractor, an unreviewed AI publisher, a general client portal, a PSA/RMM replacement, or a compliance certification. This makes the offer clearer and reduces misleading-comparison risk.

## 3. Pricing: a validation protocol, not a public rate card

Current competitor pricing spans lower-cost portal tools and higher-cost BI/reporting products; these figures do not establish willingness to pay for a new additive RipoDoc layer.[2] Do **not** publish a self-serve hosted rate, checkout, free trial, guarantee, refund promise, or auto-renewal language until the commercial decision register is complete.

| Offer | Working hypothesis | Validation requirement | Public website treatment now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founding Partner | $499/month, three months prepaid; limited onboarding, first report built together, direct founder support. | Three prepaid customers after contracts, billing/tax, cancellation/refund rules, and seller identity are approved. | “Request a report diagnostic” — no price, payment, savings, or availability claim. |
| Core | $799/month concept for a larger managed-client book. | Repeated willingness-to-pay, evidence of product use after a real report cycle, and defined scope. | Do not publish. |
| Scale | $1,499+/month concept for advanced controls/integration expectations. | Customer demand and a validated support/security model. | Do not publish. |
| Free tier | No free tier or unlimited free pilot proposed. | N/A. | Keep the public interactive sample free, clearly labelled sample data, with no account requirement. |

**Discovery questions:** Ask every prospect what they spend on reporting today, whether RipoDoc would displace any spend, where the budget would come from, and whether they will pay a three-month founding-partner commitment. A compliment, waitlist signup, or request for a custom integration is not validation.

## 4. First-customer motion

The first motion is founder-led discovery and paid design-partner work, not broad product-led acquisition. Target MSPs with 20–150 staff, 50+ managed clients, no dedicated BI team, an existing reporting process, and a buyer who can describe a recent “show me the data” moment.[3]

| Stage | Weekly operating target | Proof that advances the funnel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account research | 40 ICP accounts with a known stack or public reporting signal | Three relevant people and one account-specific observation. |
| Outreach | 120 personal contacts across those accounts | A reply to a specific report-teardown invitation, not generic interest. |
| Discovery | 5 conversations | A documented current process, pain, cost, evidence challenge, and price/budget answer. |
| Demo | 2 report teardowns | Prospect sees claim → support → review/publish boundary in their relevant narrative. |
| Close | Founding partner invitation only after fit | Prepaid, permissioned, mutually scoped work after legal/commercial gates are met. |

### The five-minute demo

1. Start with a concise sample MSP report and identify one executive-facing claim.
2. Ask, “What happens today when the client asks to see the source behind this?”
3. Select the claim and reveal a bounded evidence panel, not the entire operational export.
4. Show the human review/publish boundary and explain that AI does not authorize publication.
5. Show the controlled-delivery boundary and audit-aware record; do not claim compliance certification.
6. Ask the two pricing questions and request a report-teardown follow-up, not a vague pilot.

## 5. Six-month evidence roadmap

| Period | Primary product outcome | Commercial evidence gate | Launch decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | Public sample, diagnosis path, consent notice, and operating documentation complete. | 15 structured discovery calls; five prospects describe the same report-trust pain. | If absent, change positioning before adding features. |
| Month 1 | A partner can take PDF + structured support through review and controlled publication in a non-production proof environment. | Three prepaid founding partners only after seller, contract, and payment decisions are approved. | Invite-only design-partner launch, not public hosted launch. |
| Month 2 | Three partners complete a real report cycle with documented correction and review effort. | Each partner processes one permissioned report with an approved data path. | Continue only if the flow is used in real work. |
| Month 3 | Review/publish/evidence delivery and audit experience are usable enough for repeat cycles. | Five paying customers and measured renewal intent after the first cycle. | Add only features asked for by at least three paying customers. |
| Month 4 | Reliability, performance, template/review polish, and customer feedback improvements. | 70% of paid partners continue after one completed cycle. | If not, change problem, positioning, or price—not scope. |
| Month 5 | Generic imports/adapters, self-hosted operations, security review, final policy inputs. | Permissioned reference-story candidates and no customer-specific bespoke code. | Prepare broad launch only when legal/security gates are green. |
| Month 6 | Documented production architecture, support model, pricing/contracting, and public proof story. | Ten paying customers, retained usage, two permissioned reference stories, and verified launch controls. | Broad paid hosted launch only if all gates are met. |

## 6. Launch gates

### Safe to do now

The public sample/demo, source repository, self-hosted documentation, discovery conversations, report-teardown invitations, and a **necessary-cookie notice** can be published now, provided all content remains clear that it uses sample data and that hosted pathways are fail-closed.

### Required before accepting paid hosted customers

| Gate | Why it is not optional |
| --- | --- |
| Legal seller identity and support/privacy contact | A customer needs to know who it is contracting with and where to send legal/privacy requests. |
| Approved B2B terms, privacy notice, cookie disclosure, refund/cancellation rules, DPA, and SLA posture | These documents must match the actual commercial/product operation, not generic copy. |
| Payment, billing, tax, renewal, cancellation, and refund decisions | Do not charge customers before the charge and cancellation terms are defined. |
| Configured hosted production controls | Durable database, identity, email, anti-automation, private-worker, audit, operational ownership, retention and deletion settings must match the stated offering. |
| Security and incident process | Must include the real support/escalation owner, monitoring, access control, and customer notification process. |
| Approved subprocessor and data-region record | A privacy policy cannot safely promise locations/retention/providers that are not decided. |

### Cookie decision

RipoDoc currently identifies only security/session cookies. Publish a clearly worded essential-cookie notice and cookie-policy link. Record a local acknowledgement so the notice does not repeat. Do **not** load analytics, advertising, chat, replay, or marketing cookies through this mechanism; those categories must remain off until a provider/data/jurisdiction review supports a consent-and-withdrawal control.[4]

## 7. Brand system

The **same exact RipoDoc mark** must be present in the public navigation, mobile menu, footer, README, Open Graph image, Organization JSON-LD, canonical `logo` URL, sample-report/demo frame, self-hosted docs, and future demo video title frame. Brand visibility should never be confused with an unearned badge, certification, customer logo, review score, or external endorsement.

## 8. Success metrics to instrument once a configured production path exists

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Report-to-evidence activation | At least one claim-to-evidence interaction per published report. | Tests whether the wedge is used. |
| Review correction time | Time between draft evidence and approved publication. | Tests workflow fit without claiming automation quality. |
| Evidence access outcome | Opened, expired, revoked, or denied outcome for controlled delivery. | Tests delivery control and client use. |
| Discovery-to-paid conversion | Prepaid partner agreements divided by qualified report teardowns. | Tests pricing and ICP. |
| First-cycle renewal | Paid partners continuing after one full report cycle. | Tests durable willingness to pay. |
