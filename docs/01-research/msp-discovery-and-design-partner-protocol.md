# MSP Discovery and Paid Design-Partner Protocol

**Objective:** Convert the RipoDoc thesis into customer evidence. The program is successful only if it distinguishes a burning, budgeted workflow problem from polite interest in a clever demo.

## 1. Target participant and exclusion criteria

| Criterion | Include | Exclude |
|---|---|---|
| Company | MSP with 20–150 staff, 50+ managed clients, English-first operating team. | One-person break/fix shops, primarily retail IT, or firms with no recurring client-reporting motion. |
| Role | Owner, vCIO, account-management lead, service-delivery lead, or the person accountable for QBR/customer reporting. | A contact who can neither describe the existing workflow nor authorize a paid experiment. |
| Current workflow | Manual PDF/deck/spreadsheet, BrightGauge/Power BI/Cognition360/MSPBots/Handover/portal reporting, or a mix. | Firms that do not send a client-facing operational/strategic report. |
| Pain | Can name a recent client question, evidence dispute, report-preparation burden, or over-sharing concern. | General interest in AI without a specific report/QBR event. |
| Data path | Can provide non-sensitive sample structured data, a redacted report, or a guided mapping session. | Requires RipoDoc to ingest uncontrolled customer data before security and scope are agreed. |

## 2. Discovery sequence

The founder conducts 20-minute discovery calls before showing a full demo. The purpose is to understand the present workflow and budget source, not to convince a prospect that the feature is impressive.

| Segment | Question | Evidence captured | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current workflow | “Walk me through how you prepare the QBR or client report for your most important account.” | Tools, actors, elapsed time, manual steps, approval points. | Establishes the actual job and baseline. |
| Client challenge | “Tell me about the last time a client asked where a number came from, challenged it, or asked for the underlying detail.” | Exact moment, impact, follow-up work, customer sentiment. | Tests the central RipoDoc wedge. |
| Cost | “How many hours does your team spend per client per cycle, and who does the work?” | Estimated time, loaded role, frequency. | Provides a customer-specific value model; label as self-report. |
| Evidence and privacy | “When you share details today, what are you unwilling to send by email or give a client unrestricted access to?” | Data sensitivity, access method, audit needs. | Tests willingness to value scoped evidence controls. |
| Existing alternatives | “What tools do you already pay for, and what do they not handle in this moment?” | Stack, satisfaction, gaps, contract owner. | Prevents generic competition claims. |
| Budget source | “What do you currently spend on reporting tools per year? If this solved the problem, what would you cut or change to fund it?” | Amount/range, replacement candidate, buying path. | Resolves additive versus displacement tension. |
| Data source | “Where do the source rows live today, and what is the least painful approved way to access them?” | PSA/RMM/CSV/REST/manual mapping. | Selects onboarding and connector direction. |
| Commitment | “If we build your first controlled report with you and meet agreed success measures, would you commit to a paid three-cycle design partnership?” | Explicit yes/no, conditions, authority. | Converts discovery into truth-seeking. |

## 3. Report teardown protocol

A report teardown is the product demonstration and needs a real report-shaped moment—not a generic AI demo.

1. Ask the MSP to choose a non-sensitive sample or redacted client report.
2. Identify three claims a client could challenge: a service KPI, an asset/risk count, and an operational/compliance statement.
3. For each claim, map the source system, evidence rows, approver, intended recipient, retention/expiry expectation, and whether existing tools can answer the question in the moment.
4. Demonstrate the RipoDoc sample in this sequence: PDF figure → recipient proof → exact approved detail → approval context → expiry/revocation outcome → audit event.
5. Ask the prospect to rank the value of: faster answer, least-data sharing, review accountability, recipient control, and auditability. Do not lead the ranking.
6. Close only on a paid three-cycle scope if the existing workflow and customer pain are specific enough to measure.

## 4. Paid design-partner offer

| Element | Required structure |
|---|---|
| Scope | One MSP, a defined number of client organizations, selected report type, structured/approved input path, and three report cycles. |
| Payment | Paid up front or through a fixed paid commitment; no indefinite free pilot. The public price is not published until discovery validates additive/displacement position. |
| Founder service | Founder-led onboarding, first report build, source mapping, review setup, and a short debrief after every cycle. This is intentionally manual early work. |
| Customer obligations | Nominate an accountable owner, give non-sensitive/approved test data, record baseline preparation time, conduct the workflow, and join a review call. |
| Security | Execute appropriate data-processing/terms review before personal or client-sensitive data enters a controlled environment. Use redacted/sample data until scope is authorized. |
| Success criteria | First report delivered; evidence review completed; client recipient can use the evidence path; measured time/quality outcome; decision to continue after three cycles. |
| Product feedback | All requests are tagged by workflow stage and source system; a request does not become a roadmap commitment until repeated. |

## 5. Evidence scorecard

| Metric | Definition | Source | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interview-to-paid conversion | Paid commitments ÷ qualified discovery calls. | CRM/lead records. | Tests pain and offer clarity. |
| Time to first approved report | Verified workspace creation to approval decision. | First-party product event, no evidence content. | Tests onboarding friction. |
| Evidence completion | Recipient proof completions ÷ controlled evidence opens. | Privacy-safe aggregate event counts. | Tests client usability; not a measure of customer value alone. |
| Report preparation reduction | Customer-reported baseline hours minus post-workflow hours, per report cycle. | Signed/consented partner scorecard. | Compare by report type; do not generalize from a single customer. |
| Three-cycle retention | Partners completing three cycles ÷ partners activated. | Partner records and product event counts. | Core proof-gate metric. |
| Paid continuation | Partners paying after the design scope ÷ design partners completing the scope. | Billing/contract record. | Tests budget and value. |
| Budget source | Share naming an existing tool/process to displace versus net-new spend. | Discovery notes. | Determines positioning and pricing strategy. |
| Repeated integration demand | Same source system/workflow requested by at least three paying partners. | Structured request record. | Selects the first native integration. |
| Referral/expansion signal | Qualified introduction, broader client scope, or higher plan request. | Partner/CRM record. | Early evidence of repeatability; not an NRR claim until cohorts exist. |

## 6. Connector decision rule

No native PSA/RMM integration is built because a prospect requests it. A connector is selected only when at least three paying partners identify the same source system as the primary blocker, the data/access model is technically and contractually viable, and the expected onboarding reduction can be measured against the structured CSV/REST path.

| Decision | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Keep structured CSV/REST | Onboarding succeeds quickly and source-system requests are fragmented. |
| Build a direct connector | Three paid partners request the same system; data mapping repeats; a viable auth/scope model exists; the connector reduces first-report time or review work. |
| Defer a connector | Request is unique, procurement/API access is unclear, data quality is poor, or it creates disproportionate security/operational risk. |

## 7. 30-day founder cadence

The founder runs five discovery conversations per week, documents every call in a structured evidence sheet, performs at least two report teardowns per week, follows up within 24 hours, and reviews patterns weekly. The only weekly product decisions are: reduce time to first report, remove a recurring review burden, or solve the repeated source-data blocker. Conferences, broad partnership negotiations, and speculative integrations are deprioritized unless they produce paid design-partner conversations.
