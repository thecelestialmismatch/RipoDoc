RipoDoc / Brutal Audit B / 18 August 2026

Expand the proof path.
Do not become another MSP portal.

A decision-grade assessment of the only expansion path that reinforces RipoDoc’s current wedge: accountable evidence delivery, not platform sprawl.

Scope: RipoDoc only · US + Australia context · Prepared by Manus AI · Research and analysis, not legal, financial, or investment advice.

Executive verdict

A bounded expansion can strengthen RipoDoc; a broad expansion will make it less differentiated. The right path is to deepen controlled evidence delivery—from a proof view to an accountable delivery operation—while refusing to become a generic QBR suite, portal, data room, ticketing system, or autonomous compliance authority.

The boundary that can be defensible

Broad MSP products make QBRs easier to prepare and client information easier to access. Generic secure-document systems control entire files. RipoDoc’s only durable sentence is different: when a client challenges a material report claim, reveal reviewed, bounded support to the intended reader, under an explicit delivery policy.

Falsifiable demand hypothesis: A qualified MSP will pay to replace an unrestricted raw export with a reviewed, limited, recipient-specific proof path—and will use it again in the next report cycle.

If five MSPs do not return for a second report cycle after seeing a working pilot, this is more likely a feature than a company.

Expansion architecture and safety constraints

Stage 0 — fix production readiness first

Do not build a new feature while the live hosted workspace is intentionally fail-closed. First configure database, migrations, authentication, canonical origin, email, CAPTCHA, durable audit, private worker, founder intake, monitoring, backup/restore, and the operator runbook. The live health endpoint currently reports these services unconfigured.

Stage 1 — Evidence Delivery Receipt

Receipt fieldRequired boundary
Report and claim identityImmutable report/version and claim IDs. Never rebuild a historical delivery from mutable report state.
Evidence provenanceReviewed source, capture time, reviewer, scope statement, and manifest hash.
Recipient referenceHash or protected identifier. Never expose raw recipient addresses in broad analytics.
Policy factsIssue time, expiry, revoke/reissue events, optional watermark/export policy, and versioned policy ID.
Access outcomeGranted, denied, expired, revoked, or error only; do not collect unnecessary behaviour telemetry.
IntegrityTenant-isolated append-only or integrity-chained storage with an explicit retention policy.
Acceptance test: an operator can answer “what did we send, under which policy, with what evidence boundary, and what happened?” without opening evidence rows or guessing from logs.

Stage 2 — Delivery policy

Build a tenant-scoped, versioned delivery policy after the receipt exists. It should define recipient binding, maximum TTL, revocation authority, reissue rules, optional watermark/export deterrents, retention, and safe event endpoints. Describe browser controls as deterrence; they cannot prevent screenshots or copying.

Stage 3 — Webhooks with a zero-evidence rule

Emit only state transitions: issued, granted, denied, expired, revoked, and reissued. Never emit evidence rows, raw report content, tokens, raw recipient addresses, or secrets. Require HTTPS, host allowlists, signing, replay protection, idempotency keys, bounded retries, dead-letter handling, and a tenant kill switch.

Stage 4 — Source maps and connectors

Build no connector catalogue until at least three paid design partners request the same source system and can name its authoritative field, capture cadence, approver, scope, and error owner. The first connector should be read-only, tenant-isolated, source-specific, and human-reviewed before publication.

YC-readiness: theme adjacency is not funding evidence

YC’s current RFS includes AI-Native Compliance Infrastructure. RipoDoc is adjacent only if it builds accountable evidence operations—not if it relabels a report-linking tool as “AI compliance.” YC also says an RFS is not necessary to apply.
