# RipoDoc Launch Research: MSP Reporting, Competition, and Buyer Needs

**Prepared:** 2026-08-15  
**Scope:** Current public MSP QBR/reporting guidance, primary competitor pages, and official cookie-consent guidance. This memo distinguishes observed market signals from RipoDoc hypotheses that require paid-customer validation.

## Market finding

The market does not need another generic dashboard pitch. Public MSP material repeatedly frames the QBR as a strategic client conversation, where concise business-oriented reporting must show what the MSP did, why it matters, and what action should follow. Axcient calls for evidence of work and clear executive summaries; ScalePad contrasts strategic QBRs with long report-reading sessions; NinjaOne recommends a short business-facing template with KPI selection, business translation, and a future action plan; Guardz emphasizes preparation, transparency, KPIs, and documented follow-up.[1] [2] [3] [4]

> **RipoDoc opportunity:** preserve the concise executive report while making a selected claim answerable when a reader asks, “Show me the evidence behind this number.” This is a narrow evidence-delivery wedge, not a promise to replace a PSA, RMM, BI tool, or client portal.

| Buyer question | Evidence from public guidance | RipoDoc implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Can we demonstrate value without burying the client in operations data?” | QBR content should connect service activity to business outcomes and action plans rather than become a document dump.[1] [2] [3] | Keep the report brief; attach a bounded proof path only where challenge or follow-up is likely. |
| “Can an executive verify a claim during the meeting?” | Vendors emphasize transparency, data-backed insights, and KPI review, but report delivery approaches range from dashboards to portals to BI.[1] [4] | Demo the claim-to-evidence interaction, not generic AI generation. |
| “Can we make QBR preparation repeatable across clients?” | Standard templates, relevant metric selection, and recurring processes are recurring advice.[2] [3] | Lead with the structured source/review/publish workflow and impose no PSA/RMM replacement requirement. |
| “Can we give customers access without emailing broad, raw exports?” | CloudRadial markets on-demand client information and client portals; reporting vendors market dashboards and drill-downs.[5] [6] | Position recipient-aware evidence delivery, expiry/revocation, and an audit-aware manifest as the differentiator to validate. |

## Differentiation to test, not claim as market fact

RipoDoc should test this category statement:

> **RipoDoc is the controlled evidence-delivery layer for the report an MSP already sends: select one approved claim, reveal its bounded support to the intended recipient, and keep an audit-aware record of the delivery.**

This avoids unsupported comparison language. It also respects the existing product boundary: RipoDoc is not arbitrary PDF extraction, does not permit AI to publish without review, and should not present itself as a compliance certification.

## Pricing conclusion

The observed reporting/portal price floor is broad: CloudRadial publicly described portal plans at $95–$195/month in its 2024 article, while ConnectWise listed $335–$655/month for Reports and Dashboards and ScalePad listed Cognition360 from $449/month.[5] [6] [7] These figures are **competitor prices, not proof that an additive RipoDoc purchase can sustain a particular price.**

The internal $499 / $799 / $1,499+ ladder should remain a discovery-and-prepayment experiment, not a public checkout promise, until the owner has supplied commercial terms and the first buyers validate whether RipoDoc is additive or displaces a current reporting line item. The discovery call must ask both what the prospect pays for reporting and what they would stop paying for if RipoDoc materially improved report delivery.

## What competitors appear not to foreground

The reviewed primary pages foreground dashboards, data sources, reports, portals, client scoring, operational/financial analytics, and strategic review preparation.[5] [6] [7] In the reviewed materials, the public marketing emphasis is not a per-claim, recipient-bound evidence delivery and revocation workflow. That is a **marketing-surface observation**, not a guarantee of missing feature parity. It is enough to justify a validation question: *will MSPs pay specifically to make report claims more inspectable without broadly sharing raw operational exports?*

## Cookie and privacy implementation conclusion

RipoDoc’s currently identified cookies are authentication/session and CSRF-security cookies. The UK ICO explains that necessary cookies can fall within an exemption, but still expects clear information; it also requires affirmative consent before non-essential cookies are set.[8] The European Commission likewise distinguishes operational/authentication cookies that do not require consent from preference and analytics choices.[9] California law gives covered businesses notice and consumer-rights obligations, including an opt-out of sale or sharing where applicable.[10]

The correct product decision is therefore **not** a deceptive “accept all” banner when the site has no reviewed non-essential tracking. Implement a transparent essential-cookie notice with a persistent preference record and a future-safe control surface that defaults all optional analytics/marketing categories to off. Before any non-essential analytics, advertising, replay, pixel, or chat technology is added, identify the provider, data fields, jurisdictions, subprocessor role, consent requirement, opt-out/withdrawal behavior, and legal approval.

## Research-backed launch questions

| Question | Evidence needed before treating it as true |
| --- | --- |
| Does “show me the evidence” happen often enough to be a paid problem? | At least 15 structured discovery calls and examples of report challenges from the prospect’s actual workflow. |
| Is $499/month viable as an additive offer? | Prepaid commitments, not interest; capture what reporting spend is displaced, if any. |
| Is the report/evidence demo compelling? | Demo-to-paid conversion and actual use on a partner’s report cycle. |
| Does controlled delivery matter relative to a dashboard or portal? | Explicit willingness-to-pay and repeated feedback from multiple paying MSPs. |
| Can RipoDoc claim time savings, retention, or renewal impact? | Instrumented before/after data from permissioned customers; do not infer it from vendor case studies. |
